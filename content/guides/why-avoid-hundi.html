Hundi is an informal system: you give money to someone in Australia, and a contact pays your family in Nepal, with no licensed company involved. It can look cheaper, but it carries real risks.

It is illegal

In Nepal, sending or receiving money outside licensed channels breaks foreign exchange and anti-money-laundering laws, and authorities regularly prosecute hundi operators and their customers. In Australia, anyone offering remittance services must be registered with AUSTRAC. Using an unregistered operator can draw you into an investigation.

You have no protection

If the money doesn't arrive, there is no receipt, no complaints scheme and no way to get it back. Learn the warning signs in how to spot a remittance scam or fake agent.

It hurts Nepal

Remittances through banks bring foreign currency into Nepal's economy. Hundi takes it out of the system.
