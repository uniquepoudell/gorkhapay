Sending money out of Nepal is much more restricted than sending money in. Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB) controls foreign exchange, so you cannot simply use an app to move rupees to Australia. Here is how it normally works.

What is allowed

Individuals can send foreign currency abroad through a licensed Nepali bank or financial institution for approved purposes. Common ones for Australia are:

  • Tuition fees and living costs for students (with an offer letter or Confirmation of Enrolment).
  • Medical treatment.
  • Visa, application and exam fees.

Each purpose has its own documents and limits, and NRB updates them from time to time. Ask your bank for the current rules before you go.

Documents your bank will usually ask for

  • Your citizenship certificate and passport.
  • Proof of purpose: offer letter, CoE, invoice from the college or hospital.
  • The receiving account details in Australia (account name, BSB, account number, and the bank's SWIFT code).
  • For students: the student's visa or visa application.

Paying an Australian college

Many universities and colleges accept international payments through a payment partner listed on their fees page. These partners often accept SWIFT transfers from Nepali banks. Follow the college's instructions exactly and include the student ID as reference.

Receiving in Australia without a local bank account

If the student has not opened an Australian bank account yet, a multi-currency account such as Wise can provide AUD account details to receive the transfer. Check that the sending bank accepts the receiving details first.

What not to do

Do not pay someone in Nepal to "deliver" Australian dollars in Australia. That is hundi, and it is illegal. Read why you should never use hundi.
