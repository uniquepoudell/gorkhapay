1. Decide how your family will receive it

Most providers can pay into a Nepali bank account, a mobile wallet such as eSewa, Khalti or IME Pay, or as cash at a pickup agent. Bank deposit is usually cheapest. Cash pickup suits family without a bank account. See which payout method to choose.

2. Compare the amount received, not just the fee

Enter your amount on the comparison page. Look at how many rupees arrive, because a "zero fee" provider can still cost more through a weaker exchange rate. More in how to find the cheapest transfer.

3. Check the provider is licensed

Any business sending money from Australia must be registered with AUSTRAC. You can search AUSTRAC's Remittance Sector Register by business name or ABN. Found an "agent" on Facebook? Read how to spot a remittance scam first. In Nepal, money must be paid out by a company or bank licensed by Nepal Rastra Bank.

4. Sign up and verify your identity

You will need a passport or Australian driver licence, and sometimes proof of address. This is a legal requirement, not a sign of a problem. Larger transfers may ask where the money came from.

5. Add the receiver's details

  • Bank: full name exactly as on the account, bank name, branch and account number.
  • Wallet: the mobile number registered to eSewa, Khalti or IME Pay.
  • Cash pickup: full name as on their citizenship card. They show ID and the tracking number (often called MTCN or PIN).

6. Pay and share the tracking number

Paying by bank transfer or PayID is usually cheaper than a card. Keep the receipt and send the tracking number to your family only, never to anyone else.

Tips

  • Many providers give a better rate on your first transfer. Check promotions before you pay.
  • Sending one larger amount is usually cheaper than several small ones when there is a flat fee.
  • Rates move every day. Around Dashain and Tihar demand is high, so compare early.
